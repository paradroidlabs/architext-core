## I. The Five Pillars

These are the philosophical/thematic axes defined in the project's Continued Refinement section. All planning and prose should dramatize these through action, sensation, and structural choices — **never through stated theme.**

### Pillar 1 — Sable as humanity's honest mediated consciousness

Sable experiences reality only through mediation — transducers, translation, indirect contact. Her honesty is in her precision: she refuses to impose meaning prematurely. The tension is between honest mediation (what her body tells her) and institutional mediation (what the Archive's vocabulary allows her to say). When these diverge, the novel's central conflict activates.

### Pillar 2 — Technology / transducers as prosthetic understanding

The transducers are not sensors. They are how Sable *understands*. A supernova becomes warmth. A room becomes pressure contours. The technology doesn't just extend perception — it constitutes it. When the prosthesis reconfigures (Ch6 attunement), Sable's understanding reconfigures with it. The limits of the prosthesis are the limits of what can be known through the body.

### Pillar 3 — Coda + the Archive as civilization's worship of preserved signal

The Archive classifies, catalogs, and commodifies acoustic fossils — preserved signals from dead stars, ancient geological events. This is civilization's relationship to the past rendered as institutional practice: worship through taxonomy. The devotional fossil above the maintenance tech's door (Ch6) is the personal/sacred version. The Archive's classification system is the institutional/commercial version. Both are forms of worship. The catastrophe at Hearth (4-22-6) is what happens when worship becomes processing — when civilization tries to *contain* the signal instead of *listening* to it.

### Pillar 4 — The Resonance as context collapse, inherited grief, and signal severed from origin

The Resonance is not a disease or an attack. It is **context collapse**: the boundaries between one universe's signal and another universe's reality dissolving. The symptoms (shared false memory, material anomalies, biographical erosion) are **inherited grief** — the prior universe's dying moment propagating into the present as physical and psychological disturbance. The signal is **severed from origin** — a voice mid-sentence from a speaker who no longer exists, a message whose context has been erased. The tragedy is not the signal's content but its orphaned state.

### Pillar 5 — The ending logic as completion / correct listening rather than conquest

Every institutional response to the Anomaly is a form of conquest: classify it, contain it, suppress it, destroy it (Hearth's purge). All conquest responses have failed. The novel's endgame logic is **completion** — the signal is not a threat to be defeated but a transmission to be correctly received. Sable's trajectory is toward correct listening: not conquering the signal but completing the circuit it has been holding open for a billion years. The ending is not victory. It is understanding.

## III. Semantic Distillation Framework

### The Problem

Agent instances cannot reliably read full chapter prose (~7,500 words each) due to block rendering limitations. For thematic alignment audits, agents need a compressed, semantically rich representation of each chapter that preserves philosophical content without requiring full prose access.

### The Workflow Question

**When should distillation happen?**

The prose-generating agent should distill **immediately after writing each chapter**, as the final step of that session. This is optimal because:

1. The full chapter content is already in the model's context window
2. The agent has the blueprint context and standing orders loaded
3. No rendering issues — the content was just generated
4. It's lightweight (~500–800 words per chapter)

**This is NOT asking the model to write prose twice.** The distillation is analytical, not generative. It's closer to the review step (Phase 6) than to prose generation (Phase 4). Think of it as: the agent that just built the house is the best one to draw the as-built diagram.

### Pipeline Amendment: Phase 6.5 — Semantic Distillation

Insert after Phase 6 (Review Analysis), before next chapter blueprint:

**Input:** The chapter's prose (in context from generation) + the review analysis just completed.

**Output:** A structured distillation appended to this dock page.

**Budget:** ~500–800 words per chapter.

## VII. Meta-Reference Prevention Protocol

> **Problem:** Chapter-number references ("since Ch10," "for eleven chapters," "Ch8's measurement") bleed into prose at rates that correlate with retrospective density. Ch12 produced 19 instances across 3 correction passes — the highest by a wide margin. Post-hoc cleanup does not scale; prevention must be structural.
> 

> **Root cause:** During prose generation, chapter numbers function as compositional shorthand for referencing prior events. The more convergent/retrospective a chapter, the more shorthand leaks into output. The shorthand is useful during planning but is a fourth-wall violation in prose.
> 

### Layer 1 — Diegetic Time Map (Phase 4, mandatory blueprint section)

Every blueprint must include a **Diegetic Time Map** table that pre-maps all retrospective references the chapter will need. This gives the prose generator ready-made substitutions *before* composition begins.

**Template:**

```
DIEGETIC TIME MAP
| Reference             | Diegetic Anchor                          |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Ch1 / the capture     | "during the capture dive"                |
| Ch4 / phantom         | "since childhood" / "the phantom warmth" |
| Ch7 / column dive     | "during the column contact"              |
| Ch8 / Hearth          | "since the Hearth disclosure"            |
| "eleven chapters"     | "three seasons" / "since arrival"        |
| "for N chapters"      | "for weeks" / "for [diegetic duration]"  |
| ChN's measurement     | "the earlier measurement" / "Thresh's earlier survey" |
```

**Rules:**

- Map every prior chapter the current chapter references (check blueprint's continuity hooks, context log references, and convergence points)
- Include both event anchors ("since the Hearth disclosure") and duration anchors ("three seasons," "weeks")
- The map must cover *implicit* references too — "for eleven chapters" is a duration that needs a diegetic equivalent even if no specific chapter is named
- If a blueprint references more than 5 prior chapters, flag the chapter as **HIGH BLEED RISK** and double the verification effort in Layer 3

### Layer 2 — Standing Constraint (Phase 5, prose generation)

Add to standing orders for all prose generation:

<aside>
🚫

**ZERO chapter numbers in prose output.** All temporal, sequential, and comparative references must use diegetic time:

- Event names: "since the Hearth disclosure," "during the capture dive," "after the column contact"
- Diegetic durations: "three seasons," "weeks," "since arrival"
- Relative references: "the earlier measurement," "the first time," "when she had last"
- **Never:** "Ch[N]," "chapter [N]," "for [N] chapters," "since Ch[N]," "[N] chapters ago"

Consult the Diegetic Time Map in the blueprint before generating any retrospective passage.

</aside>

### Layer 3 — Verification Scan (Phase 5.5, post-generation / pre-review)

Formalized as a named pipeline step: **Phase 5.5 — Meta-Reference Scan.**

After prose generation, before Phase 6 review:

1. **Pattern scan** for: `Ch` followed by a digit, `chapter` followed by a digit, `for [number] chapters`, `[number] chapters`, `since Ch`, `Ch[N]'s`
2. **Contextual scan** for: any numeric reference that implies chapter count rather than in-story quantity (e.g., "for eleven" where eleven = chapter count, not diegetic time)
3. **Replace** all hits using the Diegetic Time Map
4. **Log** the count in the Phase 6 review header:
    - 0 hits → `Meta-reference scan: CLEAN`
    - 1+ hits → `Meta-reference scan: [N] corrected` + list corrections
5. If a chapter produces **5+ hits**, add a **process note** to this protocol's Incident Log (below) analyzing why and whether the Diegetic Time Map was insufficient

### Bleed Risk Indicators

Flag a chapter as **HIGH BLEED RISK** during blueprint phase if any of:

- Blueprint references events from 5+ prior chapters
- Chapter contains a retrospective/convergence sequence (e.g., Ch12's voice reactivation pulling threads across the novel)
- Chapter contains a "recontextualization" beat that reframes prior events (e.g., Ch10's four-channel midpoint)
- Chapter is in Act 3-4 where thread convergence naturally increases

For HIGH BLEED RISK chapters: expand the Diegetic Time Map to cover *every* prior chapter referenced, and perform Layer 3 scan with heightened attention to implicit references (durations, comparisons, measurements attributed to prior scenes).
