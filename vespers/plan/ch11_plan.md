### Action 4.2 — Chapter Goal, Arc & Working Title

**Primary purpose:** Thresh becomes the chapter's emotional center. His interior — inaccessible until now except through Sable's external reading of his micro-expressions — opens fully through a conversation he initiates after witnessing Senne's continued decline despite the symbol breakthrough. The chapter delivers the human cost of the signal: not the cosmic scale (Hearth's 3,600), not the institutional scale (the suppression notice), but the *personal* scale — a brother who introduces himself to his sister every day and watches her not know him.

**Secondary purpose:** Sable's cataloguing framework fails completely. She has spent ten chapters processing every experience through her institutional apparatus — file it, store it, move on. Thresh's question (*How long will you take to know?*) is the first stimulus she cannot process through any framework. Not because the question is complex but because the answer requires her to admit that she may already know how to answer the signal and is choosing not to act because acting means accepting what completion might cost. The procedural monotone breaks because the procedure has no entry for *I am afraid of what I can do*.

**Tertiary purpose:** Pick up the deferred Stage 4 elder event and Archive suppression notice from Ch10's unexecuted outline beats. The elder event + suppression notice establish Maret's institutional response operating at colony scale, parallel to Thresh's personal crisis.

**Working title:** *Thresh* — the character's name as chapter title. The only chapter in the novel named for a person other than a concept, location, or object. The title signals that this chapter belongs to him — not his POV (the novel stays in Sable's third limited), but his truth, received through Sable's mediation.

**Internal processing register:** *Helplessness/urgency* — Sable cannot file what she witnesses (Senne's decline, Thresh's grief, her own fear). The cataloguing frame does not break because of new information. It breaks because the information demands *action*, and action requires her to leave the frame.

**Specific plot points to hit:**

1. Sable returns to the medical unit days after Ch10. Senne has declined — recognition delay extended, symbol-mediated clarity gone. She does not recognize Thresh.
2. First public Stage 4 elder event (deferred from Ch10): colony elder found wandering, unable to recall own name. Archive suppression notice issued under Maret's authority.
3. Thresh initiates a conversation with Sable. He tells her what the last six days have been like. She tells him about the midpoint realization: four channels, intended receiver, completion question.
4. He asks: *Can you answer it?* She says: *I don't know yet.* He says: *How long will you take to know?*
5. Sable's log has no entry for the preceding six hours. The framework breaks.
6. Social texture: residential corridor personal objects — photographs, acoustic relics, names in colony hand.
7. Wall breach observation: inner ring now. The breach is spreading inward.

**Character arc (Sable):** From recognized recipient (Ch10) to *paralyzed actor*. She has the vocabulary. She has the four channels. She has the midpoint realization. She knows she is the intended receiver. But knowing and acting are different operations, and acting on this knowledge means diving to *respond* — not to receive, not to catalogue, but to emit, to transmit, to complete the circuit from her end. Completion may mean accepting the Resonance's full progression. She cannot file this decision because no filing system accommodates *I might need to trade my continuity for a conversation with a dead universe*.

**Character arc (Thresh):** From bridge (Ch10) to *the chapter's moral engine*. His role transforms from structural (connecting Sable to Senne) to emotional (confronting Sable with the human cost of her paralysis). He does not understand the notation, the four channels, the midpoint realization. He understands that his sister is disappearing and the person who might stop it is choosing to analyze instead of act. His question — *How long will you take to know?* — is not impatient. It is *terrified*. He has watched Senne lose another piece of herself every day since the symbol breakthrough, and the breakthrough's temporary nature makes the loss worse: he has seen his sister present and coherent, and then he has watched her leave again every time the symbols are removed. He knows recovery is possible. He knows it does not last. He is asking Sable to make it last.

**Character arc (Senne, offscreen):** Decline from Ch10's symbol-mediated clarity. Without the notation present, she returns to Stage 3→4 baseline. Recognition delay extending. The biological inheritance is consuming more of her cognitive resources. The window of clarity the symbols provided is evidence that the language *works* — but it also means the language's absence leaves her further diminished each time, like a frequency that sustains a structure while active and lets it collapse when removed.

### Action 4.4 — Granular Scene Planning

**Scene 1 — The Sixth Day**

- *Entry mechanic:* TIME JUMP. Several days after Ch10. Opens with Sable already at the medical unit's observation window — the same unit she entered for the first time in Ch10, now familiar enough that her body navigates to it without institutional purpose.
- *Purpose:* Establish Senne's decline since the symbol breakthrough. The cost of temporary clarity.
- *Key beats:*
    - Through the observation window's glass: Thresh seated across from Senne. He is leaning forward slightly — the posture of someone making themselves available, reducing the distance, presenting his face at the angle most likely to trigger recognition. His hands are still — not signing, not moving, waiting for her to see him first.
    - Senne is looking at him. The recognition delay: not three seconds (her Ch10 baseline) but longer. Seven seconds. Eight. Her eyes track his face with the soft, searching quality of someone who knows they should recognize what they are looking at and cannot locate the connection. The cognitive resources that the symbols had temporarily redirected toward clarity have been reabsorbed by the signal's architecture. Without the notation present, the language that cuts through the interference is absent, and the interference fills the space the clarity occupied.
    - Thresh speaks. Sable cannot hear him through the glass — she reads his mouth, his throat vibration transmitted faintly through the window frame: his name, her name, the familial relationship. He is introducing himself to his sister. The specific care of his articulation — slow, clear, each word given individual space — tells Sable this is practiced. He has done this before. He has done this every day.
    - Senne's face: the recognition does not arrive. The searching quality in her eyes continues for several seconds and then settles into something Sable recognizes from the institutional vocabulary: *baseline confusion, Stage 4 onset*. Senne does not know this person. She is courteous — her posture accommodates a visitor — but the accommodation is social, not personal. She is being polite to a stranger.
    - Thresh holds his composure. The four-beat knee-tap pattern (Ch6) is absent — he has moved past the scaffolding. What Sable reads in his face is not composure maintained by technique but composure maintained by *repetition* — he has done this six times and the practice of doing it has become its own structure, the way a ritual becomes its own support. He introduces himself to his sister and he waits and she does not know him and he says something else — something Sable cannot read because his head turns slightly — and then he sits back.
    - The observation window between Sable and the scene functions as the chapter's structural metaphor: Sable watching through glass, receiving through mediation, unable to reach in. She is the intended receiver of a billion-year transmission and she is standing outside a window watching a brother lose his sister to the signal she was built to decode.
    - She does not enter the room. She watches for approximately three minutes. Then she leaves.
- *Elaboration techniques:* The entire scene rendered through the observation window — Sable's mediated perception applied to an intimate human situation. Thresh's composure described not through his scaffolding techniques (which have been exhausted) but through the quality of his practiced grief. Senne's decline tracked through recognition delay timing (3 seconds in Ch10 → 8+ seconds now). The glass as structural device: Sable outside, the personal cost inside, mediation as both her gift and her limitation.
- *Estimated word count:* ~1,800

**Scene 2 — The Corridor** *(Social Texture Beat + Stage 4 Elder Event + Inner Ring Breach)*

- *Entry mechanic:* HARD CUT. Sable in the residential ring corridor, moving away from the medical unit. No transition from Scene 1.
- *Purpose:* Social texture. Stage 4 elder event aftermath. Archive suppression notice. Inner ring wall breach.
- *Key beats:*
    - The residential corridor leading away from the medical unit. Sable has walked this corridor several times now. But today she reads it differently. Not because the corridor has changed but because *she* has changed — the midpoint realization has reconfigured what she notices.
    - **Personal objects outside doors:** Photographs pinned to door frames (she has never examined them closely before — they are *photographs*, the same medium as her photograph, the medium the prior universe chose to transmit a face). Acoustic relics in hand-made brackets (the devotional fossil from Ch6, multiplied — this corridor has dozens). Names written in colony hand on small placards or painted directly onto doors — the residents' names, in their own handwriting, claiming the institutional space as personal. The corridor is the most *inhabited* space Sable has ever navigated. Not the most populated — the market would be more crowded. The most *inhabited*: every surface carrying evidence of a specific person's specific life, the way a fossil carries evidence of a specific moment's specific physics.
    - She reads the corridor through her transducers. Each bracket transfers its object's micro-oscillation to the wall, to the floor. The corridor hums with the specific aggregated vibration of dozens of personal objects, each one a relationship between a person and a relic. She is walking through a museum of personal devotion — the colony's acoustic theology expressed not in a single alcove (Ch8) but in every doorway, as fundamental as addresses.
    - **The suppression notice:** On the corridor's information display — a screen at a junction. An Archive administrative broadcast, issued under Maret's authority. Language: "Isolated cognitive event under medical investigation. No public health concern identified. Colony residents experiencing unusual memory disruption are advised to report to the medical unit for evaluation." The notice is dated two days ago. Sable reads it and understands: a Stage 4 event occurred. The Archive is managing it as a medical incident, not an environmental one. Individual pathology, not colony-wide threat. Maret's Hearth strategy replicating: containment through information architecture.
    - **The elder event (aftermath):** She does not witness the event directly — it happened two days ago. But the corridor carries its aftermath. At one door near the junction, the personal objects have been rearranged — a photograph removed, the bracket empty, a colonist's handwritten name placard turned face-down on the shelf beside the door. Someone has been taken to the medical unit. The absence reads louder than the presence: a name turned over, an identity in the process of being removed from the space it occupied.
    - **Community response:** Through the floor: the residential corridor's aggregated micro-oscillation has *increased* since her last visit. More relics, more brackets, more photographs — since the elder event, the corridor's residents have added objects to their doors. The devotional response to crisis: when the institution offers a suppression notice, the community offers more relics. More names on doors. More claims on identity. The colony is protecting itself the only way it knows how — by holding its objects closer.
    - **Inner ring wall breach (brief):** She passes a wall panel that her right hand reads differently from the adjacent panels. The anomalous material signature — the different crystal structure from Ch8's outer ring discovery. It is here now. Inner ring. Residential corridor. The prior universe's material has reached the space where people live. She notes the location and dimensions (~0.5m × 0.3m, smaller than the outer ring breaches but present). She does not stop. She carries the breach's coordinates in her body and keeps walking.
- *Elaboration techniques:* The corridor as a sensory landscape — every surface carrying personal data. The contrast between the Archive's institutional notice (clinical, managing) and the corridor's organic response (devotional, accumulating). The elder event rendered through absence rather than presence — the empty bracket, the turned-down name. Wall breach: brief, factual, alarming.
- *Estimated word count:* ~2,000

**Scene 3 — The Question**

- *Entry mechanic:* IN-MEDIAS-RES. Sable and Thresh already mid-conversation. They are in a corridor alcove or common space near the medical unit. The conversation started before the scene begins.
- *Purpose:* Thresh's interior opens. The chapter's emotional centerpiece. The urgency question.
- *Key beats:*
    - Thresh found her after the corridor walk. He knew she was at the window — he saw her through the glass when he turned his head in Scene 1 (the moment she couldn't read). He let her leave. He finished his visit with Senne. Then he found her.
    - **The six-day report.** He tells her what the last six days have been like. Not in institutional language, not in the measured signs of a professional interpreter. In the language of a brother: short sentences, specific details, the accumulated weight of repetition.
        - Day one after Ch10: Senne remembered the symbols. Asked for them. They weren't there (Sable has the only copy). Senne's clarity faded within an hour.
        - Day two: Recognition delay present but short. She called Thresh by a different name — not a random name, a name from the signal's architecture that Thresh does not recognize.
        - Day three: Delay extended. She asked where she was. Accepted the answer. Did not seem to believe it.
        - Day four: Seven seconds. She was polite. She did not know him.
        - Day five: She did not look up when he entered.
        - Day six (today): He introduced himself. She waited for him to explain who he was. He explained. She nodded. She did not ask follow-up questions. She is disappearing not toward blankness but toward the signal — what replaces her biographical memory is not nothing but the room, the warmth, the recognition. She is becoming the transmission.
    - Sable tells him what she has understood since Ch10. The four channels. The intended receiver. The completion question. She tells him because he has earned the information — because he brought Senne's marks, because he built the bridge, because his question in Ch9 (*Can you help her?*) and his facilitation in Ch10 were acts of faith in a process he doesn't understand. She tells him because she cannot hold it alone anymore.
    - His response: he does not ask about the four channels or the cosmic implications. He asks: *Can you answer it?*
    - She says: *I think so. I have three of the twelve coordinates from the notation work. I would need to reverse-engineer the remaining nine and transmit them through the transducer array.*
    - He processes this. Then: *How long?*
    - She says: *I don't know. The transmission would require a dive. The hardware wasn't built for emission — I'd be using the transducers in reverse. I don't know if the array can sustain it.*
    - He says: *How long will you take to know?*
    - The question lands. Not as a demand — as a *plea*. Thresh is not impatient. He is terrified. He has watched Senne present and coherent in the symbol window and then watched her leave again six times. Each time the window closes, she is further away. Each day the signal writes more of itself into the space where Senne used to be. He is watching biological erasure in real time and the only person who might stop it is explaining that she needs more time.
    - Sable does not have an answer. Not because the question is difficult but because the answer requires her to commit to *attempting completion*, and attempting completion means diving to transmit, and diving to transmit means accepting whatever the Resonance does to her body during the attempt. She is paralyzed by the specific fear that the signal she was built to complete will complete *her*.
    - She says: *Soon.*
    - Thresh looks at her. He reads her the way she reads him — through accumulated familiarity. He knows she is afraid. He does not say so. He signs: *She remembered the symbols. Not me. The symbols.* He pauses. *Whatever you built on that paper — it reached her when I couldn't. So whatever it costs you to finish this — she's already paying more.*
    - He leaves.
- *Elaboration techniques:* Extended dialogue scene — the chapter's longest. Thresh's six-day report delivered as compressed narrative within the conversation. The urgency question as central beat. Thresh's final line (*she's already paying more*) is the chapter's moral argument: the cost of inaction exceeds the cost of action. It should land without melodrama — Thresh is not accusing. He is *reporting*.
- *Estimated word count:* ~2,500

**Scene 4 — The Break**

- *Entry mechanic:* HARD CUT. Later — Sable's quarters. Desk. Notation sheet, photograph face-up, log terminal open. Cursor blinking on an empty entry.
- *Purpose:* Framework failure. The cataloguing apparatus breaks completely.
- *Key beats:*
    - Sable sits at her desk. The twelve symbols, the photograph, her encrypted log terminal. The log is open to a new entry. The entry is blank. The cursor blinks at the position where she would normally begin: timestamp, location, observation category, data points, interpretive notes, filing status.
    - She cannot write the entry. Not because she lacks data — she has more data than any previous session. The symbol breakthrough's temporary nature. Senne's decline. The elder event. The suppression notice. The inner ring breach. Thresh's question. Her answer (*Soon*) and the fear behind it.
    - She cannot write it because writing means filing, and filing means placing it within the framework, and the framework has no category for *I am the intended receiver of a billion-year transmission and I am choosing not to act because I am afraid of what acting will do to me while a woman I have met once and a brother I work with every day are being consumed by the signal I was built to complete*.
    - The cataloguing apparatus was designed for data — fossils, frequencies, vibrations. It was not designed for Thresh's face when he said *How long will you take to know?* It was not designed for fear.
    - She closes the log without saving. Six hours of the most significant observations she has made since the capture, and none of them filed. For the first time in three seasons, Sable has experienced something she will not reduce to data.
    - She understands: the framework was a shield. Filing, categorizing, moving on — the procedural monotone was not just her method of processing. It was her method of *not feeling*. Every observation converted to data was an observation held at arm's length.
    - She picks up the pen. Not to write in the log — to write on paper. A new sheet. Not a notation symbol — a sentence. Handwritten. The first non-institutional, non-notational, non-procedural words she has committed to paper since arriving on Coda.
    - She writes: *I am afraid of what I can do.*
    - The sentence sits on the paper. Small. Handwritten. Not filed. Not categorized. Just a person's words on a piece of paper — the same medium the prior universe chose when it needed to send a face to a child.
    - **Chapter close:** Three documents on the desk. The notation (data translated to personal grammar). The photograph (evidence recontextualized). The sentence (unmediated self-knowledge). Three stages of Sable's relationship to her own experience. The framework has broken. What replaces it is not a new framework. It is the absence of framework — the raw, unprocessed recognition that she is a person who is afraid, and that the fear is the most honest thing she has felt since she turned the photograph over.
    - She does not sleep. She sits at the desk and looks at the sentence and the twelve symbols and the face. She will dive. She knows this. The sentence on the paper is not a refusal — it is the last thing she needed to admit before she could begin.
- *Elaboration techniques:* The log terminal as structural object — cursor blinking on empty space. The progression through three documents: notation (systematic), photograph (analytical), sentence (raw). The handwritten sentence is the chapter's final image — the most unmediated moment in the novel so far. Keep it quiet.
- *Estimated word count:* ~1,800

### Action 4.6 — Pacing & Flow Plan

Ch11 is a *cost and failure* chapter — the pacing mirrors the emotional weight of what the midpoint realization means for the people around Sable. Scene 1 is *witness* (quiet, observational, the glass between Sable and the human cost). Scene 2 is *evidence* (the colony's response to Stage 4, the corridor as memorial, the institutional notice as inadequacy). Scene 3 is *confrontation* (Thresh's question as the chapter's central axis). Scene 4 is *collapse* (the framework breaks, the sentence arrives, the fear is admitted).

**Emotional shape:** witness → evidence → confrontation → collapse.

This is the inverse of Ch10's shape (revelation → elimination → synthesis → confirmation). Ch10 assembled understanding. Ch11 asks what understanding costs when you cannot act on it fast enough. The two chapters are a matched pair: the midpoint's intellectual payload (Ch10) and its emotional payload (Ch11).

Ch11 is the chapter where the novel's secondary protagonist (Thresh) receives his full characterization. He has been legible through Sable's external reading — micro-expressions, composure scaffolding, behavioral tells. In Scene 3, he speaks for himself. The six-day report and the urgency question give the reader access to Thresh's interior through his own words rather than through Sable's mediated interpretation. This is the chapter's structural gift to the character: not POV access (the novel stays in Sable's third limited) but *voice*.
