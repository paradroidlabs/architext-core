## 🧠 POV & Sensory Constraints

- **POV:** Third-person limited, anchored to Sable throughout.
- **Sable is profoundly deaf since birth.** She has never perceived sound directly.
- **Transducer system:** 7 spinal nodes + 10 fingertip nodes. Translates acoustic data to haptic sensation. *Do not describe auditory experience from her POV.* Translate everything: sound = texture, temperature, pressure, geometry.
- **Haptic vocabulary established in Ch1:**
    - Impact event = sharp ice-water pinprick
    - Stellar nursery = slow warmth, sun-warmed stone
    - Supernova = structured spiral pressure across fingerpads
    - The Anomaly = geometry; burning angles; simultaneous total-node firing; *intention*
- **Transducers run warm** from Ch1 onwards — passive hardware does not generate heat. This is a persistent symptom, not background detail.
- **Sign language:** Sable and Thresh use a modified cabin-specific system, not colony standard. Broad gestures with extra visual margin are Thresh's default when communicating with hearing parties.

The Resonance is the Anomaly's effect propagating outward. It follows a fixed internal logic — it is *playing the previous universe back* in the order of original occurrence.

## 👻 Sable's Phantom Memory

- Sable has always carried a phantom memory — warmth and texture she associates with her mother's voice. Neurologically impossible for someone born deaf.
- She has dismissed it as a processing artifact.
- **Revealed Ch4:** It is not a sound. It is a *place* — the same room the Resonance is writing into the colony's collective memory.
- **Implication:** Sable has been a receiver since birth. The transducers mapped onto a neural pathway already tuned to the Resonance. She is not just the vector — she is the *intended audience* of the voice in the fossil.
- The being from the previous universe's last impression was the same impossible love Sable associates with her mother. The phantom is not her mother. It was never her mother.

## 🚶 Pacing & Scene Transition Orders

Act 1 review identified a recurring pattern: Sable walks through corridor → reads vibration signature → arrives at scene → observes → files → walks to next scene. This rhythm dominated Ch2–3 and must not repeat in Act 2.

- **Vary scene entry mechanics.** Not every scene begins with transit. Use hard cuts, time jumps, and in-medias-res openings. At least one scene per chapter should open with Sable *already in the location*, mid-action or mid-observation.
- **Trust the reader's spatial knowledge.** The colony's layout is established. Corridor-walk connective tissue between scenes should be ≤100 words after Ch5 unless the transit itself contains plot-relevant information (e.g., new bulkheads, altered vibration signatures, encounters). Do not re-describe known environments.
- **Vibration-signature environment reads on arrival:** Once per chapter maximum, and only when the signature has *changed* from baseline in a way that advances plot or threat. Sable cataloguing a *familiar* signature is characterization in Ch1. By Ch6 it is repetition.
- **Vary who initiates scenes.** Sable should not always be the one walking toward information. Someone comes to her. She's interrupted. She's summoned. She discovers something while doing something else. Break the investigate-file-move loop.
- **Chapter opening variation.** Ch2 and Ch3 both opened with vibration readings during transit. No two consecutive chapters should open with the same structural gesture.

## 🏘️ Colony Social Texture Orders

Act 1 rendered Coda as an institution, not a community. 4,000 people live on this station and the only ones the reader meets work for the Archive. Act 2 must correct this.

- **Each chapter blueprint must specify at least one "social texture" beat** — not a full scene, but ambient environmental detail during existing scenes that shows Coda as a place people *live*, not just work. These are observed by Sable in passing. They dimension the world without breaking pacing.
- **Texture categories to draw from:** spiritual/devotional practices (especially significant — the blueprint specifies sound as religion on Coda), recreational activity, market or trade, familial/domestic life, art or music culture (the colony's economy is acoustic — this should shape its culture), food beyond the mess hall, children's lives, communal gathering.
- **The theological/spiritual register is the highest priority.** The blueprint states *"sound is religion and commerce"* and *"the Resonance is theologically catastrophic."* This dimension has zero presence in Act 1 prose. It must arrive early in Act 2 — ideally through environmental observation (a devotional space, a ritual Sable passes, a cultural artifact) before any character explicitly discusses it.
- **At least one named non-Archive character** should appear by Ch7. A colonist whose stake in the Resonance is personal/communal rather than institutional.
- **The mess hall, the market, the residential corridors** — these spaces should accumulate specific detail the way the *Meridian's* dive cabin did in Ch1. Give them objects, textures, behaviors that make them feel inhabited.
- **Chiang register:** Procedural precision. The fantastical rendered through specific measured observation. Restraint before spectacle.
- **Villeneuve register:** Environment as character. Atmospheric compression. The slow devastating recognition of pattern.
- **Bold** used extremely sparingly in prose. *Italics* for thoughts, emphasis, in-world titles.
- Dialogue in standard double quotation marks.
- Chapter pages use `# Chapter N — Title` as the first content line.
- Scene sections use `## Section Title`.
- Use `---` only for minor within-scene beats, not primary scene boundaries.
- Each chapter page: Phase 4–5 blueprint first, then prose. Never plan in the review doc.

## 🔁 Continued Refinement — Philosophical Axis

- **Core hidden-theme target:** humanity is effectively "deaf" to reality and survives through mediated translation systems. Sable is the most honest embodiment of that condition, not an exception to it.
- **Technology frame:** transducers and all other interfaces should read as prosthetic understanding — useful, intimate, but never equivalent to truth.
- **Civilizational frame:** Coda and the Archive should increasingly read as a society that mistakes preservation, classification, and transmission for comprehension.
- **Resonance frame:** treat the Resonance not only as anomaly escalation but as context collapse — signal severed from origin, inherited grief, memory without witness, meaning outliving the world that made it legible.
- **Artifact frame:** the fossilized voice should function less like a clue and more like an interrupted consciousness event / unresolved transmission trying to complete itself.
- **Resolution frame:** preserve the ending logic of completion, right relation, and correct listening over destruction or conquest.
- **Outstanding review focus:** confirm these ideas are visible at the scene, chapter, and Act 2 blueprint level without flattening characters into philosophy mouthpieces.
